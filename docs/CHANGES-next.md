# CellCoal (next version) — what changed

- Clones of different fitness: `--clones n=N,cells=...,birth=...,death=...,time=...,parent=...,fitness=...,founders=...` (options `--clone-n`, `--clone-cells`, `--clone-birth`, `--clone-death`, `--clone-time`, `--clone-parent`, `--clone-fitness`, `--clone-founders`, `--clone-sizes`; the options `-e`, `-g`, `-K` and `-L` are not used with it). The tumor is one mixed population of clones that grow at different rates (the clone 1 grows with the birth rate and the death rate of Ohtsuki and Innan, death 0 by default; the fitness of the other clones is their growth rate relative to it; `cells=` is the number of cells of the whole tumor today); the size of every clone today follows from its age, its growth and its founders, and the `-s` cells are sampled at random from the whole tumor, so the number of cells of every clone changes among replicates (`clones.tsv`; the size of every clone is also random in every replicate, a pure birth process, `sizes=random`, or `sizes=fixed`). The genealogy is the structured coalescent of the demes with a size that grows in every clone, and the coalescence rate 2 b / N(t) of Ohtsuki and Innan (Slatkin and Hudson is `birth=0.5,death=0.5-g`). Checked against a forward simulation of the whole tumor (`tests/clones_check.py`); pure birth tumors agree on the frequencies of the clones (1%) and the times (5-10%), see the manual (section 5.1.4).
- The output of the runs without `--clones` is identical to the previous version (golden outputs).
- A user tree file (`-T`, `--tree file=`) can have several trees, one per line: the replicate i uses the tree i (a file with a single tree works as before). This lets a program that simulates the tumor forward in time give CellCoal a different tree for every replicate.
- New program `cellforward` (`forward/cellforward.c`, built by `make` next to CellCoal and installed as `cellforward`): the tumor simulated forward in time by cell divisions (birth and death, stop by size or time), with random driver mutations that raise the birth rate of a clone, or clones started by the user at a given time with a given fitness (`--clone-time`, `--clone-fitness`, `--clone-parent`), and the tree of a random sample of cells written in Newick, one per replicate, for `cellcoal -T`. See `docs/manual/cellforward.md`. Checked against the coalescent of CellCoal (`tests/forward_check.py`).
- `cellforward`: `--division-mutation-rate` (mutations per cell division, added to the mutations per generation of `--mutation-rate`; the lengths of the branches count the divisions of the lineage) and `--driver-acts-on growth|birth` (default `growth`: a driver multiplies the net growth rate, as the fitness of `--clones`).
- `cellforward`: hypermutators, either clones of the user (`--clone-mutation-factor`) or cells that become hypermutators at random (`--mutator-rate`, `--mutator-factor`); the mutation rate does not need to follow the growth. The factor of every sampled cell is a new column of `clones.tsv`.
- `--clones`: the frequency of the clones can be given (`freq=`, `--clone-freq`); with it, `time=` or `fitness=` (one of them) is needed, and the other is derived from the size of the clone.

- Hidden shortcut `--wga mda|pta` (`wga:` in a configuration file; not shown in `--help` or the manual): theoretical, qualitative starting values for the options that describe an amplification method (MDA: strong uneven depth and ADO in segments of 20 kb; PTA: more even, less ADO in segments of 5 kb and fewer propagated errors; `-J` is a gamma shape, so the smaller it is the more variation between cells: MDA 2, PTA 5); any option the user gives is kept, and the values used are printed. `examples/config.hdf-repli-g.yaml` puts the measured REPLI-g values of the HDF data (4 cells, 0.2-0.8x) next to the options, for comparison only, not calibrated.
- `cellforward --max-clones N`: at most N clones with a random driver per tumor (default 0: no limit; the clones of `--clone-time` do not count).
